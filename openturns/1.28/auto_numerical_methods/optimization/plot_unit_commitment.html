Unit commitment¶

A power system made of thermal plants and of a hydro valley must meet an hourly electricity demand over a one day horizon, at the lowest cost. Each thermal plant has a minimum and a maximum power, a start-up cost, a minimum up time, a minimum down time and ramp limits; the hydro valley is a cascade of reservoirs connected by turbines, where the water turbined during an hour reaches the downstream reservoir at the end of that hour. Deciding which plants are running at each hour turns the problem into a mixed integer linear program.

This case is adapted from the notebook Unit commitment by Alice Lebrun, graduated from ENSTA Paris (class of 2024). The NetCDF instance the notebook relies on was provided for an ENSTA Paris student project and its provenance is not documented, so it is not shipped with OpenTURNS; this example uses a synthetic instance generated in its spirit: see the generate_unit_commitment_data.py script shipped with the use case.

Notations¶

The horizon has hours, the fleet thermal units, the valley reservoirs and turbines. For the thermal unit :

  • is the production cost (currency per MWh) and the start-up cost;

  • and are the minimum and maximum powers (MW);

  • and are the minimum up and down times (hours);

  • and are the maximum increase and decrease of power between two consecutive hours (MW).

For the hydro valley, is the inflow of the reservoir (volume per hour), its capacity, the capacity of the turbine (volume per hour) and its production coefficient (MW per unit of volume per hour). The turbine takes its water from the reservoir and releases it into the reservoir ; the special value denotes the outlet of the valley, where the water is lost. Finally is the demand at hour (MW).

Decision variables¶

  • : the thermal unit is on at hour ;

  • : the thermal unit starts at hour ;

  • for : the power of the thermal unit at hour , and the power delivered by the whole hydro valley at hour ;

  • : the volume of the reservoir at hour ;

  • : the flow of the turbine at hour .

Constraints¶

The demand must be met at every hour, and the power of a unit is either zero when it is off or between its bounds when it is on:

A start-up is detected by comparing the state of the unit with the previous hour, the state before the first hour being known:

The power cannot vary too fast, and a unit that has just been started (resp. stopped) must stay on (resp. off) long enough:

where is the initial power of the unit, so that a unit started at the first hour is also constrained by its ramp-up limit.

In the valley, the volume of a reservoir increases with its inflow and with the water released by the upstream turbines, and decreases with the water it turbines itself:

the initial volumes being known, and the volumes and flows are bounded by the capacities. The water released upstream during an hour reaches the downstream reservoir at the end of that hour, so it cannot be turbined there before the next hour: the outflow of a reservoir is capped by the volume it holds at the beginning of the hour and by its own inflow,

The power delivered by the valley is the power of its turbines:

The resulting program has 14 856 unknowns, 9 600 of them binary, and 31 848 constraints; 110 730 coefficients of its constraint matrix are non-zero, i.e. 0.02%. This is why the sparse form matters.

import os
import struct

import numpy as np
import openturns as ot
import openturns.experimental as otexp
import openturns.viewer as otv

Load the data¶

The four CSV files hold the thermal fleet, the reservoirs, the turbines and the demand. The gallery executes this file from its own directory without defining __file__, while the test suite runs it as a script.

try:
    here = os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__))
except NameError:
    here = os.getcwd()
data_dir = os.path.join(here, "..", "..", "..", "usecases")


def load(name):
    return np.array(ot.Sample.ImportFromCSVFile(os.path.join(data_dir, name), ","))


thermal = load("unit_commitment_thermal.csv")
reservoirs = load("unit_commitment_reservoirs.csv")
turbines = load("unit_commitment_turbines.csv")
demand = load("unit_commitment_demand.csv").ravel()

(p_min, p_max, cost, start_cost, min_up, min_down, p_init, ramp_up, ramp_down) = (
    thermal.T
)
min_up = min_up.astype(int)
min_down = min_down.astype(int)
v_max, v_init, inflow = reservoirs.T
start_arc, end_arc, f_max, gamma = turbines.T
start_arc = start_arc.astype(int)
end_arc = end_arc.astype(int)

n_hours = len(demand)
n_units = thermal.shape[0]
n_res = reservoirs.shape[0]
n_tur = turbines.shape[0]

Index the unknowns¶

Once flattened the unknowns form a single vector, addressed through shifted grids: U[t, i] is the index of , P[t, i] the index of , and so on.

t_grid = np.arange(n_hours)[:, None]
i_grid = np.arange(n_units)[None, :]
U = (t_grid * n_units + i_grid).ravel()
S = n_hours * n_units + U
P = (2 * n_hours * n_units + t_grid * (n_units + 1) + np.arange(n_units + 1)).ravel()
V = (
    2 * n_hours * n_units + n_hours * (n_units + 1) + t_grid * n_res + np.arange(n_res)
).ravel()
F = (
    2 * n_hours * n_units
    + n_hours * (n_units + 1)
    + n_hours * n_res
    + t_grid * n_tur
    + np.arange(n_tur)
).ravel()
n_var = int(F[-1] + 1)

Bounds and variable types¶

The known initial state of the thermal units is handled with bounds rather than with constraints: a unit that is running before the first hour is on at that hour and produces its initial power.

binary = ot.OptimizationProblemImplementation.BINARY
lower = np.zeros(n_var)
upper = np.full(n_var, 1e30)
finite_upper = np.zeros(n_var, dtype=bool)
types = np.zeros(n_var, dtype=int)
cost_vector = np.zeros(n_var)

upper[U] = 1.0
upper[S] = 1.0
finite_upper[U] = True
finite_upper[S] = True
types[U] = binary
types[S] = binary

u_init = (p_init > 0.0).astype(float)
running = np.flatnonzero(p_init > 0.0)
lower[P[: n_units + 1][running]] = p_init[running]
upper[P[: n_units + 1][running]] = p_init[running]
finite_upper[P[: n_units + 1][running]] = True
lower[U[:n_units][running]] = 1.0

upper[V] = np.tile(v_max, n_hours)
finite_upper[V] = True
upper[F] = np.tile(f_max, n_hours)
finite_upper[F] = True

cost_vector[P] = np.tile(np.append(cost, 0.0), n_hours)
cost_vector[S] = np.tile(start_cost, n_hours)

Assemble the constraint matrix¶

Every constraint is a row of . The rows are accumulated as (row, column, value) triplets and the matrix is built in a single call: only the non-zero coefficients are ever stored, and no dense matrix is ever allocated.

ROW, COL, VAL, LB, UB = [], [], [], [], []
INF = 1e30


def add_row(entries, low, high):
    index = len(LB)
    for column, value in entries:
        ROW.append(index)
        COL.append(int(column))
        VAL.append(float(value))
    LB.append(float(low))
    UB.append(float(high))


for t in range(n_hours):
    # demand, met by the thermal units and by the valley
    add_row(
        zip(P[t * (n_units + 1) : (t + 1) * (n_units + 1)], np.ones(n_units + 1)),
        demand[t],
        INF,
    )
    for i in range(n_units):
        power = P[t * (n_units + 1) + i]
        state = U[t * n_units + i]
        # production bounds
        add_row([(power, 1.0), (state, -p_min[i])], 0.0, INF)
        add_row([(power, 1.0), (state, -p_max[i])], -INF, 0.0)
        # start-up indicator
        if t == 0:
            add_row([(S[i], -1.0), (U[i], 1.0)], -INF, u_init[i])
        else:
            add_row(
                [
                    (S[t * n_units + i], -1.0),
                    (state, 1.0),
                    (U[(t - 1) * n_units + i], -1.0),
                ],
                -INF,
                0.0,
            )
        # ramp limits, including the transition from the initial power at the
        # first hour so that a unit started at the first hour cannot jump
        # directly to a power above its ramp-up limit
        if t == 0:
            add_row([(power, 1.0)], -INF, p_init[i] + ramp_up[i])
            add_row([(power, -1.0)], -INF, ramp_down[i] - p_init[i])
        else:
            add_row(
                [(power, 1.0), (P[(t - 1) * (n_units + 1) + i], -1.0)], -INF, ramp_up[i]
            )
            add_row(
                [(power, -1.0), (P[(t - 1) * (n_units + 1) + i], 1.0)],
                -INF,
                ramp_down[i],
            )

for i in range(n_units):
    # minimum up time: a start at hour k keeps the unit on for min_up hours,
    # or until the horizon end when the window is truncated
    for k in range(n_hours):
        length = min(min_up[i], n_hours - k)
        window = [(U[t * n_units + i], 1.0) for t in range(k, k + length)]
        if k == 0:
            add_row(window + [(U[i], -length)], -length * u_init[i], INF)
        else:
            add_row(
                window
                + [(U[k * n_units + i], -length), (U[(k - 1) * n_units + i], length)],
                0.0,
                INF,
            )
    # minimum down time: a shutdown at hour k keeps the unit off for min_down
    # hours, or until the horizon end when the window is truncated
    for k in range(n_hours):
        length = min(min_down[i], n_hours - k)
        window = [(U[t * n_units + i], 1.0) for t in range(k, k + length)]
        if k == 0:
            add_row(window + [(U[i], -length)], -INF, length * (1.0 - u_init[i]))
        else:
            add_row(
                window
                + [(U[k * n_units + i], -length), (U[(k - 1) * n_units + i], length)],
                -INF,
                length,
            )

# hydro capacities
for t in range(n_hours):
    for j in range(n_res):
        add_row([(V[t * n_res + j], 1.0)], -INF, v_max[j])
    for k in range(n_tur):
        add_row([(F[t * n_tur + k], 1.0)], -INF, f_max[k])

# water balance, the volume of the first hour being the initial one plus the
# water entering and minus the water turbined during that hour
for t in range(n_hours):
    for j in range(n_res):
        entries = [(V[t * n_res + j], 1.0)]
        balance = inflow[j] + (v_init[j] if t == 0 else 0.0)
        if t > 0:
            entries.append((V[(t - 1) * n_res + j], -1.0))
        entries += [(F[t * n_tur + k], -1.0) for k in range(n_tur) if end_arc[k] == j]
        entries += [(F[t * n_tur + k], 1.0) for k in range(n_tur) if start_arc[k] == j]
        add_row(entries, balance, balance)

# the water released by the upstream turbines reaches the reservoir at the end
# of the hour, so it is not available to the turbines of that reservoir before
# the next hour: the outflow is capped by the volume at the beginning of the
# hour plus the reservoir's own inflow, not by the same hour upstream release
for t in range(n_hours):
    for j in range(n_res):
        entries = [(F[t * n_tur + k], 1.0) for k in range(n_tur) if start_arc[k] == j]
        if not entries and t == 0:
            continue
        available = inflow[j] + (v_init[j] if t == 0 else 0.0)
        if t > 0:
            entries.append((V[(t - 1) * n_res + j], -1.0))
        add_row(entries, -INF, available)

# conversion of the turbined water into power
for t in range(n_hours):
    entries = [(P[t * (n_units + 1) + n_units], 1.0)]
    entries += [(F[t * n_tur + k], -gamma[k]) for k in range(n_tur)]
    add_row(entries, 0.0, 0.0)

A = otexp.SparseMatrix(len(LB), n_var, ot.Indices(ROW), ot.Indices(COL), ot.Point(VAL))
n_rows, n_cols, nnz = A.getNbRows(), A.getNbColumns(), A.getNbNonZeros()
print("unknowns    : %d (%d binary)" % (n_var, int(types.sum())))
print("constraints : %d" % n_rows)
print(
    "matrix      : %d rows x %d columns, %d non-zeros (%.2f%%)"
    % (n_rows, n_cols, nnz, 100.0 * nnz / (n_rows * n_cols))
)

unknowns    : 14856 (9600 binary)
constraints : 34368
matrix      : 34368 rows x 14856 columns, 125755 non-zeros (0.02%)

The size of the constraint matrix is what makes the sparse storage decisive here. A dense matrix would hold coefficients, each on 8 bytes, although 99.99% of them are zero; the compressed sparse column form stores the values, their row indices and one column pointer per column only. The indices are UnsignedInteger, i.e. unsigned long, whose width depends on the platform, so it is measured instead of assumed.

scalar_width = struct.calcsize("d")
index_width = struct.calcsize("L")
dense_memory = n_rows * n_cols * scalar_width
sparse_memory = nnz * (scalar_width + index_width) + (n_cols + 1) * index_width
print("dense form  : %.1f GiB" % (dense_memory / 2**30))
print(
    "sparse form : %.1f MiB (%.0f times less)"
    % (sparse_memory / 2**20, dense_memory / sparse_memory)
)

dense form  : 3.8 GiB
sparse form : 2.0 MiB (1917 times less)

Solve the problem¶

The linear problem receives the sparse matrix directly, and the binary variables are declared with setVariablesType() so that HiGHS runs its mixed integer solver.

bounds = ot.Interval(
    lower.tolist(), upper.tolist(), [True] * n_var, finite_upper.tolist()
)
constraint_bounds = ot.Interval(
    LB, UB, [bound > -INF for bound in LB], [bound < INF for bound in UB]
)
problem = otexp.LinearProgrammingProblem(
    cost_vector.tolist(), bounds, A, constraint_bounds
)
problem.setVariablesType(types.tolist())
algo = otexp.HiGHS(problem)
algo.run()
result = algo.getResult()
print("status      : %s" % result.getStatusMessage())
print("total cost  : %.1f" % result.getOptimalValue()[0])

status      : Optimal
total cost  : 22375139.8

Analyse the solution¶

The demand is met exactly. The thermal fleet covers most of it and the valley saves its water for the two peaks of the load curve.

x = np.array(result.getOptimalPoint())
u = x[U].reshape(n_hours, n_units)
p = x[P].reshape(n_hours, n_units + 1)
v = x[V].reshape(n_hours, n_res)
f = x[F].reshape(n_hours, n_tur)
thermal_power = p[:, :n_units].sum(axis=1)
hydro_power = p[:, n_units]
starts = np.diff(np.vstack([u_init, u]), axis=0) > 0.5
print("start-ups   : %d" % int(starts.sum()))
print("thermal     : %.0f MWh" % thermal_power.sum())
print(
    "hydro       : %.0f MWh (%.0f%% of the demand)"
    % (hydro_power.sum(), 100.0 * hydro_power.sum() / demand.sum())
)
print("demand      : %.0f MWh" % demand.sum())

start-ups   : 77
thermal     : 503731 MWh
hydro       : 18107 MWh (3% of the demand)
demand      : 521838 MWh

Two constraints are easy to violate silently, so the solution is checked against them: the first hour respects the ramp limits counted from the initial power, and no reservoir turbines more water than it holds at the beginning of the hour plus its own inflow, so that the water released upstream during an hour is only used at the next hour.

assert np.all(p[0, :n_units] - p_init <= ramp_up + 1e-4)
assert np.all(p_init - p[0, :n_units] <= ramp_down + 1e-4)
outflow = np.zeros((n_hours, n_res))
for k in range(n_tur):
    outflow[:, start_arc[k]] += f[:, k]
available = np.vstack([v_init, v[:-1]]) + inflow
assert np.all(outflow <= available + 1e-4)

The number of running units drops during the night, reaches a plateau during the day when the cheapest units are saturated, and peaks with the demand.

graph_units = ot.Graph(

The volumes show how the valley is managed: the two upstream reservoirs fill during the night and release their water from the morning to the evening, while the two lowest ones are emptied at the beginning of the day and refilled at its end.

graph_volume = ot.Graph("Reservoir volumes", "Time (h)", "Volume", True, "upper right")
